Succession guide

Estate administration: first steps.

A calm, organised start can make the legal and family questions easier to identify.

Succession involves identifying the deceased person’s estate, the people with an interest in it and the lawful authority needed to administer it.

Gather the key personal records

Start with the death certificate, identification records, marriage or family records where relevant, and any will or other testamentary document. Keep originals secure.

List assets and liabilities

Prepare an initial list of land, accounts, shares, vehicles, business interests, debts and other property. Note where supporting documents are held and whether any asset is disputed.

Identify the family and interested persons

Record the people who may have an interest in the estate and how they are connected to the deceased. Avoid distributing, selling or transferring estate property without proper authority.

Clarify the legal route

The correct application and court depend on the estate and circumstances. Disputes, prior dealings, missing documents or competing claims can affect the process. Early advice can help define the required petition, notices and supporting evidence.

Handle with care

Estate property should not be dealt with casually before lawful authority is established.
