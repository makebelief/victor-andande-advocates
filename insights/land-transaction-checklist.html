Land & property guide

Before a land transaction.

A useful starting checklist for organising documents and questions before money or signatures change hands.

A land transaction should begin with verification, not assumptions. The precise checks depend on the property, parties and proposed transaction.

Start with the parties and the property

  • Confirm the full legal identity and capacity of each party.
  • Record the property description and title details exactly as they appear on official documents.
  • Clarify whether the transaction concerns a sale, lease, transfer, charge, gift or another arrangement.
  • Identify any spouse, company, estate, trustee or representative whose consent or authority may matter.

Organise the core documents

Useful records may include title documents, identification and tax records, official searches, survey or mutation materials, agreements, consents, rates or rent information, and correspondence affecting the property. Do not rely only on copies supplied by the other party where independent verification is possible.

Look beyond the paper

Physical occupation, access, boundaries, developments, claims by relatives or neighbours, restrictions and pending disputes can all affect a transaction. A site visit and properly scoped due diligence may reveal issues that are not obvious from one document.

Agree before you pay

The written agreement should reflect the property, price, deposit, completion requirements, responsibilities and consequences of default. Obtain advice before signing or paying a material amount.

Important

This guide is general information. Property facts and legal requirements vary.
