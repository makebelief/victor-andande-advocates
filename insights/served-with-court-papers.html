Litigation guide

When court papers arrive.

Prompt, organised action helps preserve options and allows time for proper advice.

Do not ignore papers because the claim seems wrong, informal or unexpected. The document may require a response within a period set by law or by the court.

Keep the complete set

Preserve every page, attachment and envelope. Note when, where and how the papers were received and who received them. Do not mark up the original documents.

Read for dates and directions

Identify the court, case number, parties, hearing or mention date, and any stated response deadline. Different documents have different effects, so avoid assuming that one deadline applies to every process.

Collect the underlying record

Organise contracts, correspondence, receipts, photographs, notices and other records connected to the dispute. Keep electronic material in its original form where possible.

Obtain advice promptly

Early review allows an advocate to assess the documents, identify procedural requirements and advise on the available response. Avoid contacting the opposing party or making admissions before understanding the implications.

Timing matters

The correct deadline depends on the document and case. Seek advice promptly.
